How to play Tangletris

Tangletris plays like the falling-block puzzle you already know — move, rotate, clear full rows — with 17 extra shapes, a tilting 3D board and five ways to play. Here’s everything: controls, modes, scoring, speed, tips and quick answers.

Play Tangletris Today’s Daily Tangle

The goal

Pieces fall into a board 10 cells wide and 20 tall. Move and rotate them so they fill complete horizontal rows: a full row disappears and everything above drops down. Clear four rows at once and you’ve scored a Tangle. The game ends when the stack reaches the top: a new piece has no room to appear, or a piece locks with part of it sticking out above the board.

Most pieces are the seven classic tetrominoes, but the bag also deals hearts, rockets, stars and other odd shapes — more of them with every level.

On a phone or tablet

Swipe left or right on the board to move, swipe down to hard drop and tap to rotate. Or use the buttons under the board: hold, left, rotate, right, soft drop and hard drop.

The modern toolkit

  • Ghost piece: a faint outline shows exactly where the piece will land.
  • Hold: bank the current piece and swap it back later, once per piece.
  • Three previews: you always see the next three pieces.
  • Lock delay: a landed piece waits half a second before it locks, and each move or turn resets that, up to 15 times.
  • Wall kicks: a blocked rotation tries nearby positions, so pieces turn even against a wall or the stack.

Soft drop+ 1 per row
Hard drop+ 2 per row

A combo starts when you clear lines with consecutive pieces: the second clearing piece in a row is combo 1, the third combo 2, and so on — any piece that clears nothing breaks the chain. Back-to-back counts only Tangles: two Tangles with no smaller clear in between.

Example: at level 3, a Tangle straight after another Tangle, as the third clear in a row, scores 800 × 3 × 1.5 + 50 × 2 × 3 = 3,900 points.

Levels and speed

You go up a level every 10 lines. Each level cuts the time a piece takes to fall one row by 14.5% — from 800 ms at level 1 down to the 70 ms floor at level 17 — moves you to the next world in Marathon and pulls more odd shapes into the bag.

Challenge a friend

Share a result

When a game ends, press Share: you get a picture of your board in its world, a line of text and a link. In Marathon, Sprint and Chaos the link carries the game’s seed, so whoever opens it gets the same pieces — and the game shows them your score, or your Sprint time, to beat.

Collection and achievements

The game tracks which of the 24 pieces you’ve discovered and how often you’ve placed each one. Achievements mark the moments worth remembering: your first Tangle, a Tangle with an odd shape, a line cleared by the Rocket, an empty board, reaching the Inferno, a finished Sprint, a three-day Daily streak, all 24 pieces found — and more.

Tips for a higher score

  • Keep the stack flat and leave one clean well on the side for the Line.
  • Bank an awkward shape in the hold slot instead of forcing it into a bad spot.
  • Clearing lines on consecutive drops builds a combo — every extra clear in the chain is worth more, and the chime climbs higher.
  • Four lines at once is a Tangle, and chaining two in a row multiplies the reward by 1.5.
  • Watch the ghost piece before you commit; hard drop only when the landing spot is right — it’s worth twice the points of a soft drop.
  • Sweeping the board completely empty pays a huge perfect-clear bonus: 2,000 × level.
  • When the music grows a nervous ticking and the frame glows red, the stack is near the top — dig out before anything else.
  • Leave a one-cell dip somewhere: the Plus, Heart, Rocket and Tree all need one, and they’re much nastier on a perfectly flat stack.
  • Read the three previews. A rare shape is announced three pieces ahead — time enough to build the spot it needs or empty your hold slot for it.
  • Save the Dot and Domino for repairs: they plug the single gaps that nothing else fits.
  • Use the lock delay: after a piece lands you have half a second, renewed with every move (up to 15 times), to slide it under an overhang.

Frequently asked questions

Is Tangletris free?

Yes. Tangletris is free to play in your browser, with no sign-up and nothing to download. It’s also open source under the MIT licence.

Can I play Tangletris on my phone?

Yes, in any current mobile browser with WebGL 2. Swipe to move, swipe down to drop and tap to rotate — or use the buttons under the board.

Can I install it and play offline?

Yes. Choose Install in Chrome or Edge, or Share → Add to Home Screen in Safari. Installed, Tangletris opens like an app and keeps working without a connection.

Is this Tetris?

No. Tangletris is an independent Tetris-style falling-block game, not affiliated with The Tetris Company. The core idea — clearing full rows — is the same, but 17 of its 24 pieces are odd shapes the classic game doesn’t have, and it adds a tilting 3D board, six worlds and generative music.

What is the Daily Tangle?

A three-minute game with the same pieces for everyone, refreshed at local midnight. Each day has its own tangle level, a streak counts the days you play in a row, and you can share your result. See the Daily Tangle page.

How does challenging a friend work?

After a game, press Share. In Marathon, Sprint and Chaos the link carries the game’s seed, so your friend gets the same pieces and sees your score — or Sprint time — to beat.

Where is my score saved?

In your browser’s local storage, on this device only. There are no accounts and no servers: your bests, Daily streak, piece collection and achievements stay with you — and clearing the site’s data resets them.

Why does it need WebGL 2, and what if it lags?

The board and the worlds are real-time 3D. Tangletris adapts its quality automatically — antialiasing, then resolution, then bloom — to keep frames smooth. If it still feels heavy, press V to turn off the board’s tilt and motion.

How is the music made?

It’s generated live in your browser with the Web Audio API — there are no recordings at all. Each world has its own track; melodies and fills are regenerated every loop, the tempo rises with your level and the sound effects play in key.

How many pieces are there?

24: the seven classic tetrominoes plus 17 odd shapes, from the one-cell Dot to the eight-cell Rocket. See them all, with tips, on the pieces page.

That’s all you need

The rest you’ll learn the first time a Rocket drops in. It’s free, it runs in your browser, and it’s one click away.
